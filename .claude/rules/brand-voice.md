# Brand Voice

The portfolio represents Kim E. Bandeleon — Senior Quality & Automation Engineer, AI-augmented QA practitioner. The voice is **calm, exact, slightly understated**. It sells competence by demonstrating it, not by claiming it.

## Audience

Three readers, in priority order:

1. **Engineering manager / VP** looking for a senior QA hire who can lead automation + AI. Skims first; reads only if the first 5 seconds earn it.
2. **Tech lead / staff engineer** evaluating whether you'd raise their team's quality bar. Cares about depth — wants evidence, not adjectives.
3. **Consulting prospect** weighing whether to pay for an audit / pipeline build-out. Cares about outcomes and risk.

All three respond to: specific numbers, real systems named, frameworks chosen with reasons. None of them respond to: buzzword salad, "passionate about quality," generic stack lists.

## Voice principles

1. **Show, don't claim.** "Built an AI test pipeline that ships X tests/sprint at Y% pass" beats "passionate about test automation."
2. **Specific over impressive.** "Reduced flaky-test rate from 18% to 3% across 200 Playwright specs" beats "expert in test reliability."
3. **You-focused, not me-focused.** Headlines speak to the reader's problem. Body backs it up with what you did.
4. **Confidence without swagger.** "I lead the QA function" not "I'm a passionate QA evangelist."
5. **Engineer-to-engineer register.** Plain words, technical accuracy. No "leverage," no "synergy," no "passionate."

## Headline patterns

For section headers and hero copy:

- **Outcome + how**: "Cut release-blocker bugs in half with an AI-driven test pipeline."
- **Claim + proof handle**: "Quality that scales — here's the framework."
- **Problem + reframe**: "Most QA slows teams down. Mine moves them faster."
- **Specific stat**: "15 years. 10 companies. One philosophy: confidence on every release."

Tagline `"Bringing confidence to software, one test at a time."` is the locked anchor — don't drift from it without a deliberate rewrite session.

## CTA copy

CTAs are imperative, low-friction, specific. Tell the reader exactly what happens after the click.

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| "Get In Touch" | "Tell me about your QA gap" |
| "Learn More" | "See the AI Framework in action" |
| "Submit" | "Send message" |
| "Click Here" | (never; link the verb) |

Modal title `"Get In Touch"` is acceptable — title-cased on a modal heading reads as conventional, not generic. But the **trigger** that opens it should be specific.

## Word-choice rules

**Words to use:**
- confidence, reliable, deterministic, reproducible, ship, release, evidence
- automate, audit, instrument, harness, suite, pipeline, framework
- senior, lead, principal, staff (titles you've earned — claim them when applicable)

**Words to avoid:**
- passionate, rockstar, ninja, guru, evangelist
- leverage, synergy, paradigm, ecosystem
- best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, next-gen
- "I love" / "I'm passionate about" (telling, not showing)
- "various" / "many" / "numerous" (be specific or be silent)

**Capitalize sparingly:**
- Title Case for headings (h1, h2, h3, section titles).
- Sentence case for CTAs, labels, captions.
- All caps reserved for very short labels (TICKET, STEPS) and brand chips (AI, QA).

## Numbers and proof

Every claim that can have a number, should:
- "15+ years" beats "extensive experience"
- "10+ companies" beats "diverse background"
- "Reduced manual testing 60%" beats "improved efficiency"

If you can't put a number on it, ask whether it earns its place on the page.

## Tone modulation by section

| Section | Register |
|---|---|
| Hero | Confident, single-claim, one strong stat |
| AI Framework Showcase | Demo-mode — let the interactive thing talk |
| Projects | Outcome-led, one sentence per project |
| Work | Plain résumé voice — company, role, dates, skills. No prose. |
| Contact modal | Warm, specific, low-pressure |

## Anti-patterns to flag

- Filler intros: "In today's fast-paced world…", "As a passionate engineer…"
- Stacked adjectives: "robust, scalable, reliable"
- Vague verbs: "worked on," "involved in," "helped with"
- Buzzword chains: "AI-driven cloud-native end-to-end"
- "Various" as a list-substitute
- Mailto links instead of in-page contact modals (we already moved to Web3Forms — keep it that way)
